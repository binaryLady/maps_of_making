## What Went Well

1. **Map is live with real data** — 606 seeded spaces, canonical namespace, full field coverage. The foundation is solid.
2. **Integration testing discipline from Epic 0 carried through** — SPARQL PREFIX pattern was documented at the moment of discovery (Story 0.3 code review) and never caused a repeat failure in Epic 1.
3. **Tech debt scrubbed rather than deferred** — namespace migration (mapsofmaking.eu → canonical nicolasdb.github.io) was caught in Story 1.5 and resolved immediately rather than pushed forward. Zero data loss.
4. **Architectural decisions documented in sprint-status** — Nanobot deferral, canonical namespace rationale are in the right place and will inform Epic 6 without re-derivation.
5. **Code review quality** — GeoJSON compliance, graceful failure, coordinate filtering, `open_now` stub — all caught and fixed before ship.

---

## What Was Hard / Challenges

1. **Fog-of-war assumptions surface mid-story** — namespace mismatch and Nanobot image availability were discovered during execution, not planning. Both were resolved without blocking, but required unplanned work.

2. **Three overlapping handoff locations** — Story files (Dev Notes), MEMORY.md, and sprint-status cross-epic section all carry handoff information. No lifecycle on items (consumed vs. still-relevant). Story creation has no mandatory triage step to surface the right items at the right moment.

3. **Stale assumptions still reach stories** — The `:z` SELinux flag example: documented in memory and story debug logs, yet still caused friction because story creation didn't pull it in. The information existed; the trigger to surface it didn't.

4. **Deferred-work.md has no routing** — Items in `deferred-work.md` don't reference which story or epic will handle them. Items get orphaned. Nicolas carries the mental burden of tracking what goes where.

5. **Sprint-status cross-epic section accumulates noise** — Consumed items (SPARQL PREFIX, HTTP client choice) remain alongside still-relevant items, eroding trust in the signal.

---

## Key Insights

- **Fog-of-war is the correct mental model for this project** — Nicolas named it explicitly: "I'm learning here, there is much I don't know yet." UX in particular will find clarity by walking the talk, not by upfront spec.
- The antidote to fog-of-war isn't better upfront planning — it's **disciplined handoff at the moment of writing** and **mandatory triage at story creation time**.
- Nicolas's own space submission as the Epic 2 final acceptance test is the right forcing function for UX clarity.
- Coordinator hosting diversity (same-host file, GitHub Gist, Google Drive, Nextcloud, institutional IT) is a real onboarding friction that Epic 2 UX must surface, not paper over.
